## Narrative
Prior public data offers only weak, indirect support for this plan: the search surfaced several mouse liver RNA-seq datasets, but none delivered a usable high-fat-diet vs normal-chow contrast on reanalysis, so the feasibility read is **cautionary**. The closest matches by keyword are GSE306578, GSE289033, and GSE313258 (the last titled around hepatic lipid metabolism remodeling), with GSE302447 and GSE302445 ranking lower due to a major tissue mismatch. When these were reanalyzed, every candidate fell through: two (GSE302447, GSE334406) had no usable per-study counts table, and the rest could not be assigned a valid case/control grouping — for example, GSE289033 turned out to be an osteocyte-EV/antagomir study, GSE313258 a compound dose-response study, and the GSE30244x series were tofacitinib-on-HFD drug experiments with no normal-chow arm at all. As a result, no differential-expression counts or effect-size priors could be estimated from prior data. Several datasets also carried QC flags for low median library size (<1M counts) typical of low-input/EV preps, and general pitfalls include normalization choice and batch-condition confounding. Your planned N per group was not specified; as a rough guide, bulk RNA-seq typically needs about 6–12 per group to detect moderate effects. Overall, the biological question is reasonable and matching datasets exist by topic, but because no public study here yielded a runnable HFD-vs-chow contrast, I'd treat the supporting evidence as unconfirmed and consider generating your own well-balanced diet arms rather than relying on these accessions.
